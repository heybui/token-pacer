States

One model, eight states, two surfaces. The bar row is flat — no shell, no background of its own — and straddles the notch: the progress mark and the exact percentage in the left wing, the time left in the right, the hardware between them and nothing else. The drop panel is the pill shell, which grows downward out of the notch and spans past it; every expanded state lives there and leads with the same mark, scaled up. Switch the mark with the mark tweak and every state follows. Nothing is ever drawn where the hardware is.

Multiple models mode

Every provider gets the same bar: 0–100% of its own quota, 100% being spent. What differs is the clock behind it — Claude a rolling 5-hour window, Codex a week, Copilot a month — so each row carries its reset period and each marker sits wherever that provider actually is. The three capsules work untouched.

Twelve progress marks, compared

The capsule bar and the ring wings lead the list so the incumbents are judged on the same terms as the rest. Each one encodes progress by a different mechanism — liquid level, count, angle, trend, depletion, height, type — so they're worth judging against each other rather than tuning one. Nothing here reuses a metaphor macOS already owns in that strip, which is what ruled out the battery and the signal bars. All drawn at menu-bar size and shown at 58%, 81% and 94% so the zone crossings are visible. Each one is animated on its own mechanism to signal that a model is working right now — the next increment charges, a surface bobs, a grain falls, a shadow creeps — never a single shared blink. Turn it off with the working tweak to see the resting mark.

Running border · twelve variants

For the panel that hangs below the notch, an edge treatment that says a model is working right now — separate from the mark, which says how much is left. The top edge carries no effect at all: it meets the notch, so anything lit there fights the hardware. What's left differs in what travels and where — one head or two, the whole palette or a single tint, three edges or only the quiet ones, and two that don't travel at all. Every one takes its colour from the zone the panel is actually in, so the edge and the mark can never disagree — drag the chaseAt tweak past 75 and 90 to watch all twelve turn together.

Preferences · two panes

General holds everything numeric and behavioural — the zone boundaries, the sound, launch at login, hiding when nothing is running. Appearance holds the two choices made by eye. Both lists there are twelve items long, which rules out a popup menu: a name like “Eclipse” tells you nothing about what will sit in your menu bar. So each becomes a grid of the real thing at real size — mark tiles drawn at menu-bar scale, border tiles as actual panels hanging from the tile edge, animating. It is live: click any tile.
